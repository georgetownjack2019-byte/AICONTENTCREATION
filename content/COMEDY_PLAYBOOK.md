# Comedy playbook: pacing, reactions, effects

Every session reads this before planning a video, together with `REMAKE_RULES.md`.
Built from reference videos the user chose. **Borrow techniques only, never characters, designs or looks.**

---

## Reference 1: RED (Xiaohongshu) @叉烧猫 (38.4K): "当女朋友说要减肥时" ("When your girlfriend says she's going on a diet")
48 s file: 45.5 s of story + an outro card. 3D cartoon cat couple (her: fluffy cat with a "减肥" (diet) headband; him: round cat).

### Story shape: escalation ladder
Setup gag → temptation 1 (cake) → she resists → temptation 2 (noodles) → resists harder → temptation 3
(delivery tower of desserts) → full dessert table → she breaks ("忍不住了", "I can't hold it") → smoke-cloud food fight →
aftermath: both bloated on the floor, sign "明天再减" ("diet tomorrow") → final button: "那再来一顿" ("one more meal then") + BBQ grill.
**Each temptation is bigger than the last, and the payoff is the opposite of the premise.**

### Pacing
- About 26 beats in 45.5 s, so **one beat every ~1.75 s**; shots are often 0.5–1.5 s.
- Gag in the **first 2 s**: she lifts a barbell and her eyes shoot fire (2.0 s), then she flies off a treadmill (3.0–4.5 s).
- No dead air. The audio stays dense the whole time (RMS about −7 to −15 dB: music + voice + SFX).
- **Silence before the punchline:** the audio drops to about −23 to −27 dB for 0.5–1 s right before big reveals
  (e.g. just before the bowl smash and the cake-on-head shock), then slams back.

### Reactions (the funny part)
- **Extreme, cartoon-impossible faces:** eyes on fire, heart eyes, glowing red eyes, full eye-roll whites ("白眼"),
  tongue-out despair, drool, saucer eyes, a jaw drop.
- **Meltdown moment gets its own world:** a close-up despair face on a purple dramatic backdrop with "我干了什么!!"
  ("What have I done!!"). The background swaps to sell the emotion.
- **Thought bubbles:** a cloud shows the imagined fit, muscular self, so we see the *motivation* in one image.
- **Extreme close-ups of the temptation:** food fills the whole frame (fried egg, chicken, fries) so the craving is felt.
- **Physical gag props:** a mallet bonk ("都怪你", "it's your fault"), a bowl slammed onto a head, a cake tower balanced on the head,
  donuts stuck all over the body, a smoke-cloud fight with limbs flying.
- **Body exaggeration as payoff:** both end up as round bloated balls on the floor.

### Text
- **Title hook** at the top for the first ~0.5–1 s: the premise ("When your girlfriend says she's going on a diet").
- **Bracketed stage directions** at the bottom, like narrator commentary: "(悠哉路过)" ("strolls past casually"),
  "(减肥失败啦!!)" ("diet failed!!"), "(白眼)" ("eye roll"). Dry, deadpan narration is half the joke.
- **Short spoken/subtitled lines** of 2–5 words: "要吃吗" ("want some?"), "减什么减" ("diet? what diet?"), "不行不行" ("no no no"), "第二顿!" ("second meal!").
- Cute **sign props** carry the final joke ("明天再减", "diet tomorrow").

### Camera
- Mostly static framings, with a cut on every beat instead of camera moves.
- Mix of wide (the room, the dessert table), medium (both characters) and **big close-ups** (faces and food).

## Rules for Kenji & Yuki (apply to every video)
1. **Gag in the first 2 s** (a reaction face or a mini-disaster), with the premise as title text.
2. **One beat every 1–2 s.** Cut on every beat; no shot longer than ~2.5 s unless it's a deliberate hold.
3. **Escalate:** each repetition bigger than the last; end on the opposite of the premise plus a final button.
4. **Reactions at 200%:** make dedicated *reaction stills* (fire/heart/glowing eyes, eye-roll whites, saucer eyes,
   tongue-out despair, jaw drop) and hold them 0.5–1 s with a snap zoom and a sound hit. Don't rely on the video model for big faces.
5. **Meltdown backdrop:** for the biggest emotional beat, swap to a dramatic plain/purple/spotlight background close-up.
6. **Thought bubble** to show what a character wants or imagines.
7. **Temptation and reveal close-ups:** fill the frame with the object of desire or the reveal.
8. **Prop gags** over complex motion (a mallet, a bowl on the head, things stuck to the body, a smoke-cloud fight).
   They're funnier AND safer from glitches than real physical action. Show the before/after with a cut, not the in-between motion.
9. **Text layers:** title hook (top, start) · short dialogue lines (2–5 words) · **bracketed deadpan narration**
   at the bottom, e.g. "(Kenji pretends he's fine)", "(diet failed)" · a sign prop for the button.
10. **Sound:** dense music + SFX on every beat; **0.5–1 s of silence right before each punchline**, then a hit.
11. **Length:** trend remakes follow the original's length (REMAKE_RULES). Original stories may run 20–45 s *only* if
    they keep the 1–2 s beat rate; otherwise aim for 8–15 s.

## Glitch-safe production (with these rules)
- Most beats = **stills with a zoom/push-in** + SFX; animate only small motions (blink, head turn, sip, chew, gesture), 2–3 s clips.
- Big actions (falls, fights, throwing) = before still → smoke poof / smash cut → after still.
- Check every clip frame by frame; cut before any face warp.
