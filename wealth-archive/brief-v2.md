# The Wealth Archive: brief v2 (changes from v1 marked ★)

Viral documentary-style short videos telling true, jaw-dropping stories about money, wealth and power, for a curious English-speaking audience with no finance background. One real, fact-checked story per video, never repeated.

## Story selection (unchanged, plus ★)
Shocking number, extreme rise or fall, underdog, scandal / scam / fraud, crazy bet, a famous company's secret origin or near-death moment, crash or bubble, bizarre money fact, or a lesson that changes how people think about money. Strong contrast; rotate billionaires, companies, crashes, scams, money psychology, history's richest.
- ★ **Famous name or brand in the first 3 seconds** (Nike, McDonald's, Apple, Elon Musk, Nintendo...). People stop for names they know, and the names make the video searchable.
- ★ **Test before scaling:** each week, note which categories got the most watch time and shares, and do more of those.

## Relevance to today (unchanged)
Current event, or a past story that mirrors today (AI, big tech, crypto, housing, inflation, viral brands). One short line connecting the story to today or to the viewer's life.

## Hook (unchanged, plus ★)
Shocking number / extreme contrast / impossible-but-true statement / burning question / start in the most dramatic moment. Never names-dates-background first. The hook makes a promise the video pays off.
- ★ **The hook is also on screen from frame 1** as big text (8 words max), and the **first image shows the hook** (the number, the collapse, the empty vault). Most viewers watch muted.
- ★ **The first shot already moves** (a ticker crashing, papers flying, a door slamming) within the first second.

## Retention (unchanged, plus ★)
Curiosity loop in the first 5 s, closed near the end; a twist every 10–15 s; concrete numbers, vivid details, simple comparisons.
- ★ **A new shot every 1.5–3 s.** Pace comes from editing (cuts, punch-in zooms, sound hits), not from camera shake.
- ★ **Word-by-word animated subtitles** on every line, big and centered in the lower third, with key numbers highlighted.
- ★ **Sound design:** a tension music bed, a riser before each twist, a hit on each big number.

## Structure ★ (shorter)
Hook → "This is the Wealth Archive." (★ see below) → quick context → rising stakes → twist → consequence → one money lesson → ending.
- ★ **Length 30–50 s** (max 60). New accounts lose most viewers after 20–30 s; shorter videos get rewatched and pushed.
- ★ **Big stories that don't fit in 50 s become Part 1 / Part 2** (see Follower growth).

## Ending (unchanged, ★ timing)
Two short lines, under 30 words in total: (1) the inspiring "keep learning / follow" line, (2) a simple opinion question linked to the story. Vary the wording every time; no episode numbers.
- ★ **The ending takes 4 s max**, so it doesn't feel like an ad.

## Tone (unchanged)
Calm, confident documentary narrator: curious, slightly dramatic, never hype, never salesy.
- ★ **Always the same narrator voice** (deep, warm, documentary). The voice is the brand.

## Visuals ★ (story, not mood)
Cinematic and realistic, like a high-end documentary; period-accurate clothing, places and technology; objects shown accurately; no cartoons or comedy props.
- ★ **Show the story happening, not a mood board.** Every shot depicts the exact moment being narrated (the signature, the crash, the bankrupt office, the line outside the bank), with **people in action and visible emotion** (hands shaking, a crowd panicking, a man staring at a ticker). Avoid generic skylines, vaults and posed businessmen; use them only as quick transitions.
- ★ **Real people:** never a realistic AI face of a real person. Show them from behind, in silhouette, by their hands, or through newspapers, documents and logos of the time. Dramatized extras (crowds, traders, workers) can show faces.
- ★ **Archive identity:** a light film grain or period color grade, plus a small "ARCHIVE FILE · 1929" stamp (year and place) on the first shot. This makes the grid recognizable at a glance.
- ★ **Cover template:** a strong frame with one huge title (e.g. "$0 → $40B", "LOST $8B IN 2 DAYS"), in the same font and color every time.

## Motion (★ adjusted)
Stable, smooth camera, steady as on a tripod; no shaking, jitter, flicker or warping.
- ★ **Stable but not static:** slow push-ins and pans inside each shot, plus fast cuts between shots (see Retention).

## Accuracy & safety (unchanged)
Verified, publicly reported facts only; say "around" or "about" for uncertain figures; never invent quotes; no rumors or speculation on private lives; no buy or sell advice, return promises, specific stock, crypto or product recommendations, or price predictions. ★ Keep the AI label on every post.

## Caption (unchanged)
Curiosity-driven, searchable title with the main name, one comment question, 3–5 hashtags.

## Follower growth (★ adjusted)
- Part 1 / Part 2 for the most dramatic stories: Part 1 ends on the cliffhanger with "Follow for Part 2"; Part 2 opens with a one-sentence recap. ★ **Post Part 2 within 24 h** and pin Part 1 on the profile meanwhile.
- ★ **"This is the Wealth Archive." stays once per video, but as a 1-second visual stamp (title card + sound hit) right after the hook, with the voice saying it fast.** Never a slow pause between the hook and the story.
- Loop the ending question back to the hook when possible; occasionally invite viewers to save the video.
- ★ **Post as a Trial Reel first on Instagram**, one video a day, with a trending sound at 5–10% under the narration.
